\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chp:conclusions}
This final chapter draws the thesis together. It first revisits the three research questions and the answers the work provides, then sets out the most promising directions for future development.

\section{Conclusion}
This thesis set out to address a clear gap in the digital tools available for arithmetic education in primary schools. Classroom exercise tools provide the engagement, adaptivity, and child-friendly design that keep children motivated, but lack the data tracking, experimental control and flexibility needed for scientific research. Research tools, on the other hand, provide the scientific infrastructure but lack the motivational qualities needed to keep children engaged across multiple classroom sessions. No existing tool covered both sides. RekenRangers was built to fill that gap.
\\ \\
Three research questions structured the work:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{RQ1}] Can a single web-based platform satisfy both the exercise-tool properties and the research-tool properties identified in this thesis?
\item[\textbf{RQ2}] Can the platform support a real multi-session classroom experiment with over 100 participants across multiple conditions, producing complete and reliable data, keeping children engaged, and running an adaptive difficulty system that performs as designed?
\item[\textbf{RQ3}] How do children evaluate the engagement and user friendliness of the platform, and how do teachers and researchers evaluate the ease of setup?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Answer to RQ1}
RekenRangers brings the twelve properties together in a single tool, but not all of them to the same extent. Figure~\ref{fig:final-grid} summarises how the platform delivers on each property. Data tracking, experimental control, reproducibility, engagement, accessibility, and user friendliness are fully realised: the tool collects complete and structured data, gives researchers fine-grained control over every aspect of an experiment, supports reproducibility through JSON export and import, offers a coherent and motivating game environment, runs in any browser without installation, and presents an interface that children can operate independently. Personalisation, adaptivity, reliability, and feedback are present and functional but could be extended. Personalisation lacks an avatar system, the adaptive algorithm requires further calibration, the feedback system is minimal, and the reliability of the platform, while sufficient for the classroom deployment, was affected by bugs and a server downtime incident that revealed room for improvement. Flexibility remains the clearest limitation: the tool supports a wide range of arithmetic studies through its settings and sequence system, but cannot match the open-ended configurability of a programming language. Ease of setup is more difficult to assess. The configuration is performed entirely without code, which is a major step forward, but the interface itself needs further work and an evaluation with researchers who were not involved in the development. In short: RekenRangers shows that bringing both worlds together is possible, but the trade-off between flexibility and ease of setup remains a real one, and several of the twelve properties leave room for improvement.

\paragraph{Answer to RQ2}
For the most part, yes. RekenRangers supported a multi-session classroom experiment with 107 participants across four conditions and four training days, deployed in seven primary schools. The platform collected 21,522 exercise records, the data was structured and exportable without preprocessing, and no data was lost despite a brief server downtime incident during the deployment. The engagement data shows that children spent 98.5\% of their earned coins voluntarily and that 99\% of participants completed at least one puzzle. The adaptive system differentiated between ability levels, but it did not reach its target accuracy, hit the rating ceiling for 14 children, and left a sizeable subgroup oscillating around the zone 3--4 boundary. These results demonstrate that RekenRangers can carry a real study from setup to data export and keep children engaged throughout, but they also point to clear calibration work that the adaptive system still needs.

\paragraph{Answer to RQ3}
The two researchers who used the tool, Eline T'joen and Kiara Willems, evaluated the ease of setup positively, particularly in comparison to their previous experience with OpenSesame and PsychoPy. The setup was manageable, the data was easy to retrieve, and the tool ran reliably during the classroom sessions. The most important concern, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, is that both researchers were closely involved in the development of the tool, which limits the generalisability of their feedback.

From the children's side, the questionnaire results indicate that both engagement and user friendliness were evaluated positively, while the perceived match between the exercises and the children's ability level remains an area for improvement.

\paragraph{Closing remarks}
RekenRangers does not aim to replace either category of existing tools entirely. Its purpose is to offer an alternative for arithmetic studies that need to take place in real classroom settings, where engagement, ecological validity, and data collection have to coexist. The work presented here shows that this combination is achievable, while making the remaining trade-offs and open problems explicit. Whether RekenRangers becomes a genuine instrument for the educational research community depends on how those open problems are addressed. The next section outlines the most important directions for future work.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future-work}
Several directions for future work follow directly from the limitations identified in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

The most important next step is to evaluate RekenRangers in terms of actual learning outcomes. The current thesis demonstrated that the tool collects clean data and keeps children engaged, but it could not show whether using RekenRangers produces measurable improvements in arithmetic skills or metacognitive awareness. A dedicated learning study, ideally with a control condition using a more traditional tool, would close this gap. The data from the deployment by Eline T'joen and Kiara Willems will provide a first answer once their thesis is completed.

Several of the twelve properties should be extended. Personalisation deserves a visible avatar system and a personal statistics dashboard, both of which the existing account infrastructure already supports. The feedback system could move beyond a binary correct/incorrect signal toward short explanations or strategy hints. The adaptive algorithm needs further parameter tuning, a more gradual transition between difficulty zones, and a scoring function with a more generous equilibrium. It would also benefit from an item-level Elo model in the style of Rekentuin \cite{klinkenberg2011computer}, together with a deeper exercise pool to remove the ceiling effect observed in the deployment.

The flexibility of the tool remains its most significant constraint. Future work should explore ways to expand the range of supported experimental designs without reintroducing the programming barrier. Possible directions include a domain-specific configuration language, a modular block system that allows researchers to define new exercise types declaratively, or the use of generative AI to translate a researcher's natural-language description of a study into the appropriate configuration. Each of these comes with its own trade-offs and is worth investigating.

A meaningful external evaluation is also needed. The current feedback comes exclusively from researchers who were involved in the development. Testing the tool with researchers and teachers who have no prior connection to the project would give a much clearer picture of its real-world usability and would likely reveal ease of setup issues that the current evaluation could not detect.

Finally, if RekenRangers is to grow beyond a research prototype, the underlying infrastructure needs to be brought to a more professional level. This includes a cleaner and better-documented codebase, a more robust hosting setup, automated testing, proper user account recovery flows, and the kind of monitoring and maintenance procedures that any production-grade educational platform requires. These are not glamorous engineering tasks, but they are what separates a working prototype from a tool that researchers and schools can rely on.
